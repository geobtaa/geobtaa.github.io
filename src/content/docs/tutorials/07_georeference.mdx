## Introduction

Georeferencing is a commonly used tool to accurately digitize data on a paper map. It uses a series of control points in the digital image to associate this image with spatial locations. The digital image could be an aerial photograph, a scanned map, or a picture of a topographic map. The georeferenced map can be used for basic map analysis, such as calculating distances and areas. In this tutorial, you will learn how to georeference a historical map by using ArcGIS Pro.

## Download Data

<Steps>
1. Visit the [BTAA Geoportal](https://geo.btaa.org) and search with the keywords `Hyde Park Community`.

2. Open the result titled "Hyde Park Community /prepared by the Department of Sociology, the University of Chicago."

3. Click the down arrow at the top right of the image preview box and select a resolution to download.

    <OneThirdLayout>
     ![Download image](@images/tut7-hyde-iiif.png)*Download an image*
    </OneThirdLayout>

4. Right click the map and save the image as “G4104-C6-2H9-1920z-U5.jpg”
</Steps>

## Data Processing

<Steps>
1. Open ArcGIS Pro
   
2. Click **Add Basemap** and choose Streets map
   
3. Zoom in to the study area: Hyde Park, Chicago, Illinois
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Add Basemap](@images/basemap.png)*Add Basemap and Zoom to Hyde Park*
    </OneThirdLayout>

4. Click **Add Data** and choose the image “G4104-C6-2H9-1920z-U5.jpg”
   
5. Click **Imagery** and Georeference.
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Georeference](@images/tut7-fit-georeference.png)*Georeference Toolbar*
    </OneThirdLayout>
   
6. Click **Fit To Display** to show the image in the study area.
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Fit Display](@images/tut7-fit-display.png)*Fit Scanned Map to Display*
    </OneThirdLayout>

7. Right click Layers -> Properties and select **WGS_1984_Web_Mercator_Auxiliary_Sphere** as the coordinate system. 
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![View Coordinate System](@images/tut7-view-coordsys.png)*View Coordinate System*
    </OneThirdLayout>

8. Explore ways to navigate the map by clicking **View** and **Navigator**, if not turned on previously.

9.  Click **Add Control Points** to select control points. Select control points from the scanned map and then choose the corresponding location on the street map.
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Example Control Points](@images/tut7-control-points.png)*Example Control Points*
    </OneThirdLayout>

10. Select control points in the area close to the four corners of the map.
    
11. Select additional control points. The more points you assign the more accurate your georeferenced map will be.
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![More Control Points](@images/tut7-point-diagram.png)*More Control Points*
    </OneThirdLayout>

    :::tip
        Here are some tips for choosing control points:

        - The number of control points needed depends on the image being used. Normally, at least four control points are required for georeferencing.
        - Choose road crossings or sidewalk intersections, because the edges of roads may change over time.
        - The control points should be spread across the unreferenced image.
    :::

12. Click **View Link Table**. It is up to you to determine the acceptable residual values. If a link has a residual value much larger than others, the link should be deleted. 
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Control Points](@images/tut7-controlpts-table.png)*Control Points Table and RMSE*
    </OneThirdLayout>

13. After georeferencing, click **Save** to save the result.
    
14. Click **Raster Layer**, change transparency to 50%. It is now easier to compare the georeferenced map locations with the real world locations.
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Transparency](@images/tut7-transparency.png)*Make Map Overlay Semi-Transparent*
    </OneThirdLayout>

</Steps>

## Publish the Map on ArcGIS Online

This topic is optional. To publish a hosted layer, you will need publishing privileges in your ArcGIS organizational account.

:::Caution
	
Publishing a tiled service to ArcGIS Online will consume credits, which are the currency used across ArcGIS. To estimate how many credits you will need to perform specific transactions or store data, you can refer to this website: https://doc.arcgis.com/en/arcgis-online/administer/credits.htm
:::

<Steps>

1. Sign in with your university account.

2. Remove Basemap

3. Click **Share**, **Web Layer**, then **Publish Web Layer**
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Share Options](@images/tut7-share-options.png)*Share Options*
    </OneThirdLayout>

4. Enter information for Item details. For Layer type, select **Tile**. 
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Share Map](@images/tut7-share-layer.png)*Share Map as a Web Layer*
    </OneThirdLayout>

5. Select appropriate levels of detail. Do not choose extreme large cashe size, which may consume all your credits.

6. Click the **Publish** button to publish this service.

7. View map in ArcGIS Online
    <OneThirdLayout>
    ![Map Layer](@images/tut7-open-arcgis-online.png)*Map Layer in ArcGIS Online*
    </OneThirdLayout>

</Steps>
